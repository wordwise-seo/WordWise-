How fast should you reply to a new enquiry?

By Ben Adamson·13 years in the removals industry·Wordwise
← FirstReply

Short answer: faster than you think, and almost certainly faster than you do now. I spent 13 years in the removals trade before I started Wordwise, and the jobs we lost were rarely lost on price. They were lost because someone else picked up first.

The honest answer most people do not want to hear

If you want a number, aim to reply within five minutes. Not five hours, not by the end of the day, five minutes. That sounds extreme until you remember how you behave as a customer yourself. When you need something done, you message a couple of firms, and you go with whoever comes back to you sounding switched on and available. You are not sitting by the phone hoping the first one you contacted eventually calls. Your customers are exactly the same.

The drop-off is steep. A reply in the first few minutes lands while the person is still thinking about the job, still on your website, still in the mood to sort it. An hour later they have moved on with their day. The next morning they have already booked someone else. The enquiry was never really yours to lose, it was yours to answer quickly or hand to a competitor.

What actually happens after someone enquires

Here is the part most business owners never see, because by the time they call back the trail has gone cold. This is roughly how a typical enquiry plays out when you take a few hours to respond.

0 min
They send the enquiry. Interest is at its absolute peak. They want this sorted and they are ready to talk.
2 min
They message two more firms. Most people never contact just one. They line up a few and wait to see who answers.
8 min
A competitor replies. The customer is now in a conversation with someone else, getting their questions answered.
25 min
That competitor has built trust. They feel looked after. You are still a name on a form they half remember.
3 hours
You finally call back. They are polite, but they have already as good as decided. The job was won two and a half hours ago.

None of that is about you being bad at your job. You were probably out doing your actual work, which is exactly the problem. The enquiry came in while your hands were full, and by the time you were free it was too late.

In removals, the firm that called back first won the move more often than the firm that was cheapest. I watched it happen for years.

Ben Adamson, Wordwise

But you cannot drop everything every time

This is the real bind for a local business. The advice to reply in five minutes is correct, and it is also impossible when you are up a ladder, under a sink, or halfway through carrying a wardrobe down a staircase. You cannot stop a job to answer your phone, and you should not have to.

That is the gap. The enquiry needs answering now, but you are not free now. Trying to solve it by sheer willpower, by promising yourself you will check your phone more often, does not work. You will always be busy when the good enquiries land, because the good enquiries land all day.

What a good first reply actually needs to do

The first reply does not need to close the job. It just needs to hold the customer until you can get to them properly. A good holding reply does four simple things:

  • Confirms you have received their enquiry, so they stop wondering
  • Reassures them you will be in touch shortly to sort it
  • Asks one useful question that moves the job forward
  • Sounds like a real person at a local firm, not a robot

Get those four things to the customer within seconds and you have done the most important part. You have stopped them drifting to the next firm on their list. You buy yourself the time to call back properly when you are off the job, and when you do, they already know you are on it.
