Why redesign

When a redesign is the right move

Not every tired-looking website needs rebuilding. But when any of these are true, a redesign usually pays for itself quickly through the enquiries it recovers.

📱

Broken on mobile

Over 60% of local searches happen on a phone. If your site is hard to use on mobile, you are losing enquiries every day.

📉

No enquiries

Visitors arrive but never call or message. Usually a problem with structure, calls to action, and clarity, not traffic.

🐌

Slow to load

Anything over three seconds loses a large share of visitors before they even see the page. Speed is fixable.

🔍

Invisible on Google

No location in your titles, no local content, no service area pages. A redesign builds these in from the start.

🏚️

Looks dated

An old design quietly costs you trust. A clean, modern site signals a business that is active and professional.

🔒

Stuck on a platform

Locked into Wix or Squarespace fees you cannot escape. A clean rebuild gives you a site you actually own.

The big risk

Your rankings come with you

This is where most website redesigns go wrong. A business spends years building up Google rankings, then a careless rebuild changes every page address and wipes all of it out overnight. The new site starts from zero and it can take months to recover.

Every Wordwise redesign starts with a full audit of what your current site ranks for in Google Search Console. Any page address that changes gets a proper 301 redirect from day one, so the ranking value moves to the new page instead of disappearing. The content that is already working is kept and improved, not deleted. Your rankings come with you.

What gets protected and improved
Existing Google rankings 301 redirects on changed pages Pages already getting traffic Customer reviews and testimonials Search Console history Your domain authority
The process

How a Wordwise redesign works

1

Audit and plan

I review your current site, note every page that is getting traffic, and record which keywords you already rank for. Nothing is changed until this is done.

2

Build the new site

Every page is rewritten with locally targeted copy, correct titles and meta descriptions, a clear call to action, and dedicated pages for each area you serve.

3

Protect the rankings

301 redirects are set up for any page address that has changed, so your existing Google rankings transfer to the new site rather than being lost.

4

Review and launch

You review the new site and request any changes. Once approved it goes live, the new sitemap is submitted to Google, and everything is checked on mobile.
